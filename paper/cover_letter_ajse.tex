\opening{Dear Professor ElAli,}

Please consider my manuscript, ``\textbf{ShiftGuard: Risk-Controlled
Spatiotemporal Traffic Forecasting under Compound Regime Shifts and Sensor
Outages},'' for publication as an Original Research Article in the
\textit{Arabian Journal for Science and Engineering}, within AJSE-Engineering
(Computer Science and Engineering / Systems Engineering).

Reliable traffic prediction is central to intelligent transportation systems,
yet most established forecasting benchmarks evaluate models under relatively
stable traffic conditions and assume that sensor observations remain
available. The submitted work addresses the more operationally realistic case
in which traffic regimes change while sensors experience independent, burst,
contiguous-region, or high-centrality outages. It asks not only whether a model
can forecast accurately, but also whether it can quantify uncertainty and
identify forecast origins at which automated decisions should be withheld.

The manuscript makes three main contributions. First, it defines a
leakage-controlled compound-shift evaluation protocol spanning two established
traffic benchmarks, METR-LA and PEMS-BAY, four outage families, five severities,
and five random seeds. Second, it develops ShiftGuard, which integrates
reliability-conditioned graph-temporal encoding, structured-outage training,
probabilistic scale estimation, and prequential group-adaptive conformal
calibration. Third, it introduces an origin-level risk certificate that converts
calibrated uncertainty into an operational release-or-abstain decision.

The empirical study comprises 640 confirmatory stress evaluations and compares
ShiftGuard with classical predictors and widely used neural baselines,
including DCRNN, STGCN, Graph WaveNet, AGCRN, STID, and STAEformer. Relative to
the strongest locally reproduced transformer, ShiftGuard reduces mean stress
MAE from 3.74 to 3.40 mph on METR-LA and from 2.49 to 1.94 mph on PEMS-BAY. At
30\% outage severity, the risk controller retains approximately 86\% and 98\%
of forecast origins while limiting empirical origin-level failure to 8\% and
2\%, respectively. The manuscript also reports the important boundary of the
claim: ShiftGuard is effectively tied with STAEformer on clean METR-LA and is
0.027 mph worse on clean PEMS-BAY. Thus, the contribution is robust and
risk-aware operation under compound shifts, rather than an unsupported claim
of universal clean-data superiority.

This work fits AJSE's scope in computer science, systems engineering, and
transportation-oriented engineering applications by combining a technically
new forecasting and risk-control pipeline with reproducible evaluation and
clear operational implications. The complete implementation is publicly
available at \href{https://github.com/shishirbit/ShiftGuard-ITS}{github.com/shishirbit/ShiftGuard-ITS},
and the manuscript provides official access information for both datasets.

I confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration by another journal. I am the sole author and
have approved its submission. The study uses previously published benchmark
data and involves no human participants, human samples, or animals. All
reported claims are supported by the submitted manuscript and reproducibility
materials.

Thank you for considering this work. I believe it will interest AJSE readers
working in intelligent transportation, spatiotemporal machine learning,
reliable artificial intelligence, and engineering decision support.
